# Research charter

## Objective

Test whether a small, stable cognitive kernel can accumulate useful capability through
external memory, expert packages, tools, skills, and hardware-adaptive execution without
making full-model retraining the default adaptation mechanism.

## Scientific rules

1. Every experiment starts with a falsifiable hypothesis.
2. Baselines and failure criteria are fixed before execution.
3. Results distinguish observations, interpretations, and speculation.
4. Negative and null results are published.
5. Conclusions are scoped to the tested configuration.
6. Source claims use primary sources whenever possible.
7. A result is not promoted because it is novel or desirable.

## Autonomy rules

The autonomous research loop may:

- survey primary research;
- design protocols and synthetic fixtures;
- write or test local analysis code;
- use already-approved, zero-cost execution paths;
- publish sanitized aggregate results;
- open follow-up research questions.

It may not autonomously:

- expose private infrastructure or data;
- start, stop, or reconfigure production services;
- download model weights or large datasets;
- train or fine-tune models;
- run unbounded compute;
- weaken safety checks;
- publish raw logs;
- promote a result without its declared acceptance criteria.

Capabilities outside the default boundary require a narrow, recorded approval for one
experiment. Approval for one experiment never becomes a general permission.

## Separation of concerns

Morpheus separates:

- **priors**: stable architecture and learned general capabilities;
- **knowledge**: replaceable evidence-backed claims;
- **experience**: trajectories, outcomes, and lessons;
- **skills**: executable, versioned procedures;
- **specialization**: optional parametric adapters or micro-experts;
- **morphology**: execution decisions derived from an anonymous capability envelope.

This separation is itself the primary hypothesis under test.
